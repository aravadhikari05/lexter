1 Structure and Use of Citations (p.65)
=======================================

Provide citations to authorities so that readers may identify and find those authorities for future research. Citations are made in citation sentences and clauses ([rule 1.1](1_1_citation_sentences_and_clauses.md)) and are introduced by signals. Signals organize authorities and show how those authorities support or relate to a proposition given in the text ([rule 1.2](1_2_introductory_signals.md)). Citation sentences and clauses may contain more than one signal. Order signals according to [rule 1.3](1_3_order_of_signals.md). Within each signal, arrange authorities according to [rule 1.4](1_4_order_of_authorities_within_each_signal.md). Parentheticals may be necessary to explain the relevance of a particular authority to the proposition given in the text ([rule 1.5](1_5_parenthetical_information.md)). Certain additional information, specific to that authority, may also be appended according to [rule 1.6](1_6_related_authority.md).
