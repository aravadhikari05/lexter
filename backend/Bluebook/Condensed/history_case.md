# Prior and Subsequent History — Condensed Rules

## B10.1.6 — Prior or Subsequent History (Bluepages)

- Full citation should include prior or subsequent history (subject to exceptions in Rule 10.7)
- Use abbreviated explanatory phrases from T8 to introduce history; italicize them
- Common phrases: *aff'd*, *aff'g*, *cert. denied* (subject to 2-year limit), *cert. granted*, *rev'd*, *rev'd on other grounds*
  - *Gucci Am., Inc. v. Gold Ctr. Jewelry, 997 F. Supp. 399 (S.D.N.Y. 1998), rev'd, 158 F.3d 631 (2d Cir.).*
  - *Cooper v. Dupnik, 924 F.2d 1520, 1530--31 (9th Cir. 1991) (holding that police officers' actions did not rise to level of due process violation), rev'd en banc, 963 F.2d 1220 (9th Cir. 1992).*
- Explanatory parentheticals should immediately precede subsequent history
- When case has different name in subsequent history: provide new name preceded by "*sub nom.*"
- No need for new name if parties merely reversed or if history is denial of certiorari/rehearing
  - *Great W. United Corp. v. Kidwell, 577 F.2d 1256 (5th Cir. 1978), rev'd sub nom., Leroy v. Great W. United Corp., 443 U.S. 173 (1979).*

## Rule 10.7 — Prior and Subsequent History

### General rules
- Give entire subsequent history when citing in full
- **Omit:** denials of certiorari (unless decision < 2 years old or denial particularly relevant)
- **Omit:** history on remand, denial of rehearing (unless relevant)
- **Omit:** any disposition withdrawn by deciding authority (e.g., affirmance followed by reversal on rehearing)
  - *Cent. Ill. Pub. Serv. Co. v. Westervelt, 342 N.E.2d 463 (Ill. App. Ct. 1976), aff'd, 367 N.E.2d 661 (Ill. 1977).* (cert. denied omitted)
  - *Cheng v. GAF Corp., 631 F.2d 1052 (2d Cir. 1980), vacated, 450 U.S. 903 (1981).* (remand omitted)
- Give prior history only if significant to cited point or if cited disposition does not intelligibly describe the issues

### 10.7.1 — Explanatory Phrases and Weight of Authority

#### (a) Prior or subsequent history
- Append to primary citation; introduce each with italicized explanatory phrase
  - *Cooper v. Dupnik, 924 F.2d 1520, 1530 & n.20 (9th Cir. 1991), rev'd en banc, 963 F.2d 1220 (9th Cir. 1992).*
- If subsequent disposition in same year as primary citation: omit year from primary parenthetical
- Chain subsequent history with additional explanatory phrases:
  - *Herbert v. Lando, 73 F.R.D. 387 (S.D.N.Y.), rev'd, 568 F.2d 974 (2d Cir. 1977), rev'd, 441 U.S. 153 (1979).*
- Prior + subsequent: give prior history first:
  - *Kubrick v. United States, 581 F.2d 1092 (3d Cir. 1978), aff'g 435 F. Supp. 166 (E.D. Pa. 1977), rev'd, 444 U.S. 111 (1979).*
- History follows any parenthetical information

#### (b) Significance of disposition
- Give reason if disposition does not carry normal substantive significance:
  - *vacated as moot,*
  - *appeal dismissed per stipulation,*

#### (c) Overruled, abrogated, and superseded cases

##### (i) Overruled
- Later decision by same court (or court with appellate jurisdiction) explicitly repudiates: *overruled by*
  - *Nat'l League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976), overruled by Garcia v. San Antonio Metro. Transit Auth., 469 U.S. 528 (1985).*
- Multiple overruling decisions: connect with italicized *and*

##### (ii) Abrogated
- Effectively but not explicitly overruled/departed from: *abrogated by*
  - *Ahrens v. Clark, 335 U.S. 188 (1948), abrogated by Braden v. 30th Jud. Cir. Ct., 410 U.S. 484 (1973).*

##### (iii) Superseded by statute or constitutional amendment
- Statute/amendment enacted to reverse outcome: cite statute + later recognizing case
  - *Wards Cove Packing Co. v. Atonio, 490 U.S. 642 (1989), superseded by statute, Civil Rights Act of 1991, Pub. L. No. 102-166, 105 Stat. 1074, as recognized in Raytheon Co. v. Hernandez, 540 U.S. 44 (2003).*
- Cite to session laws where possible

#### (d) Slave cases
- Enslaved person as party: *(enslaved party)*
- Enslaved person as subject but not party: *(enslaved person at issue)*
  - *Dred Scott v. Sandford, 60 U.S. (19 How.) 393 (1857) (enslaved party), superseded by constitutional amendment, U.S. Const. amend. XIV.*

#### (e) Multiple dispositions
- Multiple dispositions by same court: connect with italicized *and*
  - *United States v. Baxter, 492 F.2d 150 (9th Cir.), cert. dismissed, 414 U.S. 801 (1973), and cert. denied, 416 U.S. 940 (1974).*

### 10.7.2 — Different Case Name on Appeal

#### (a) Name changes in subsequent history
- Different name: give new name introduced by "*sub nom.*,"
  - *Great W. United Corp. v. Kidwell, 577 F.2d 1256 (5th Cir. 1978), rev'd sub nom., Leroy v. Great W. United Corp., 443 U.S. 173 (1979).*
- Applies even for slight differences (e.g., change in procedural phrases)

#### (b) Name changes in prior history
- Use same form:
  - *Rederi v. Isbrandtsen Co., 342 U.S. 950 (1952) (per curiam), aff'g by an equally divided court Isbrandtsen Co. v. United States, 96 F. Supp. 883 (S.D.N.Y. 1951).*

#### (c) Exceptions — do NOT provide different case name when:
- Parties' names merely reversed
- Citation is to denial of certiorari or rehearing
- In appeal of administrative action, private party name stays the same
- Change is purely stylistic (e.g., "State" to "California")
