# Parenthetical Information — Condensed Rules

## B10.1.5 — Weight of Authority and Explanatory Parentheticals (Bluepages)

- Add parenthetical after date parenthetical to indicate weight of authority
- Indicate concurring or dissenting opinions
  - *Bush v. Gore, 531 U.S. 98, 144 (2000) (5-4 decision) (Breyer, J., dissenting) ("The Establishment Clause...").*
  - *Zuni Pub. Sch. Dist. v. Dep't of Educ., 550 U.S. 81, 113 (2007) (Scalia, J., dissenting).*
  - *Webb v. Baxter Healthcare Corp., 57 F.3d 1067 (4th Cir. 1995) (unpublished table decision).*
  - *Wersba v. Seiler, 393 F.2d 937 (3d Cir. 1968) (per curiam).*
- **Explanatory parenthetical:** briefly explains the proposition for which the case stands
  - *Green v. Georgia, 442 U.S. 95, 97 (1979) (per curiam) (holding that exclusion of relevant evidence at a sentencing hearing constitutes denial of due process).*
- Explanatory parenthetical information should immediately precede subsequent case history

## Rule 10.6 — Parenthetical Information Regarding Cases

### 10.6.1 — Weight of Authority

#### (a) Generally
- Weight of authority info (en banc; in banc; 2-1 decision; mem.; per curiam; Brandeis, J.; unpublished table decision) added in separate parenthetical after date of decision
  - *Webb v. Baxter Healthcare Corp., 57 F.3d 1067 (4th Cir. 1995) (unpublished table decision).*
- When citing for a proposition that is NOT the single clear holding of a majority: indicate parenthetically (alternative holding; by implication; dictum; dissenting opinion; plurality opinion; holding unclear)
  - *Parker v. Randolph, 442 U.S. 62, 84 (1979) (Stevens, J., dissenting).*
  - *Garcia v. San Antonio Metro. Transit Auth., 469 U.S. 528, 570 (1985) (5-4 decision) (Powell, J., dissenting).*
- Related authority or history that can be expressed with a T8 explanatory phrase should NOT be given parenthetically:
  - Correct: *Wersba v. Seiler, 263 F. Supp. 838, 843 (E.D. Pa. 1967), aff'd per curiam, 393 F.2d 937 (3d Cir. 1968).*
  - Not parenthetical form

#### (b) "Mem." and "per curiam"
- "mem." = memorandum; use ONLY if court disposition issued without an opinion; do NOT use for opinions denominated "memorandum decision"
- "per curiam" = opinion issued by the court as institution (not by a particular judge); use when court so denominates it

#### (c) Seriatim opinions
- Early U.S. Supreme Court opinions where each Justice wrote separately: use *(opinion of Lastname, J.)*

### 10.6.2 — In-Chambers Opinions
- Supreme Court Justice ruling on stay/bail/injunction "in chambers": use *(Lastname, J., in chambers)*
- Pre-1969: cite Rapp volume if not in U.S. Reports
  - *Sacco v. Massachusetts, 1 Rapp 16 (1927) (Holmes, J., in chambers).*
- Post-1969: cite U.S. Reports
  - *Maryland v. King, 567 U.S. 1301 (2012) (Roberts, C.J., in chambers).*

### 10.6.3 — Quoting/Citing Parentheticals
- When cited case itself quotes/cites another case: use "quoting" or "citing" parenthetical
- Same typeface, pincite, and short form rules apply to the quoted/cited authority
  - *Zadvydas v. Davis, 533 U.S. 678, 719 (2001) (Kennedy, J., dissenting) (citing Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei, 345 U.S. 206 (1953)).*
- Only one level of recursion required; additional level optional if particularly relevant

### 10.6.4 — Order of Parentheticals
- Order: (i) weight of authority; (ii) "quoting"/"citing" parentheticals; (iii) explanatory parentheticals
  - *Wolf v. Colorado, 338 U.S. 25, 47 (1949) (Rutledge, J., dissenting) (rejecting the Court's conception of the exclusionary rule), aff'g 187 P.2d 926 (Colo. 1947), overruled by Mapp v. Ohio, 367 U.S. 643 (1961).*
- If explanatory parenthetical contains text needing a quoting/citing parenthetical, nest them:
  - *Kansas v. Crane, 534 U.S. 407, 409 (2002) ("[T]he statutory criterion... 'mental abnormality or personality disorder' satisfied '"substantive" due process requirements.'" (quoting Kansas v. Hendricks, 521 U.S. 346, 356 (1997))).*
  - *Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448, 519 (1980) (Marshall, J., concurring) (noting that conventional strict scrutiny is "strict in theory, but fatal in fact" (citing Regents of the Univ. of Cal. v. Bakke, 438 U.S. 265, 362 (1978))).*
